# Maine's Coastal Bluffs

**View the story map → https://greaterportlandcog.github.io/Maine-Coastal-Bluff/**

An interactive, scroll-through guide to the Maine Geological Survey's (MGS) 2026 update of its coastal bluff maps. It explains what coastal bluffs are, how MGS rates their stability, where bluffs are eroding fastest, and what property owners and communities can do about it.

## What's inside

- **By the numbers:** how many miles of Maine's coast are bluffs, and how much is unstable
- **Bluffs 101:** the factors that make a bluff erode: slope, shape, vegetation, shoreline, water, and sea-level rise
- **How bluffs are rated:** the 2026 stability score, with a calculator you can try
- **The 2026 map:** an interactive statewide map. Click any bluff segment to see its score.
- **High risk:** how bluff stability relates to the type of shoreline below it
- **Land use:** where groundwater and runoff are weakening bluffs, and simple steps that help
- **Case studies and resources:** links to the full data, the MGS map viewer, and planting guides

## Who it's for

Municipal planners, conservation commissions, coastal landowners, and anyone curious about how Maine's shoreline is changing.

> **Note:** This is a planning tool for broad areas. It is **not** a site-specific assessment. For a specific property, consult a licensed geologist or geotechnical engineer.

## Data sources

- **Coastal bluff data:** [Maine Geological Survey, Coastal Bluffs](https://www.maine.gov/dacf/mgs/pubs/digital/bluffs.htm). The map reads the live MGS service, so it always shows the current published data.
- **Download the data:** [MGS Open Data](https://mgs-maine.opendata.arcgis.com/datasets/4bfd3ddb425c4bb6801affb51b6afe9a_0)
- **Basemap:** Esri, HERE, Garmin, © OpenStreetMap contributors
